## Chapter 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Background

Education institutions routinely collect student demographic, attendance and assessment records. Turning such records into defensible decisions requires more than charts: it requires a documented chain from raw data to cleaned data, to analysis, to recommendations. This project implements that chain as a reproducible pipeline with fixed random seeds, pinned package versions, a logging trail for every cleaning decision, and a comparison of each model with a baseline.

### 1.2 Statement of the Problem

The raw record contains 508 rows and 12 variables [E1]. It is not directly analysable: it includes 8 exact duplicate rows, missing values in three numeric columns (15 study hours, 25 attendance, 20 prior GPA), impossible values (negative study hours down to -5.0 and attendance up to 110.0), and inconsistent category labels (8 gender labels, 9 parental-education labels, 7 extracurricular labels) [E1]. The problem is therefore twofold: first, produce a trustworthy cleaned dataset through auditable decisions; second, use it to answer specific questions about the association between study behaviours and final exam performance while reporting effect sizes, uncertainty and limitations honestly.

### 1.3 Objectives

1. Define the problem and five answerable research questions.
2. Document, clean and transform the data with every decision logged.
3. Perform exploratory data analysis with descriptive statistics, distributions, correlations and group comparisons.
4. Apply hypothesis tests and predictive models appropriate to the questions, with baselines and cross-validation.
5. Build an interactive Streamlit dashboard with more than five Plotly charts.
6. Write a five-chapter report in which every figure traces to executed code.
7. Prepare a presentation outline and defence material grounded in the actual results.

### 1.4 Scope and Limitations

The scope is one synthetic dataset of 500 unique student records after cleaning. The analysis is observational and cross-sectional. Limitations are material: the data are generated, not collected from people; 72 cells were imputed with medians; subgroup sizes are small in some categories (PhD n=37, Distinction n=1); the midterm score nearly duplicates the outcome and is excluded from conclusions; and clustering shows weak separation. No causal claim is supportable.

### 1.5 Significance

The project demonstrates academic-standard practice rather than a real educational discovery: reproducibility, privacy handling, baseline comparison, effect-size reporting and explicit disclosure of weak results. These are the practices required to defend an analysis orally.

## Chapter 2 Dataset Description

### 2.1 Source

The dataset is **synthetic**, generated by `03_Source_Code/generate_raw_dataset.py` with `numpy.random.default_rng(42)` and written once to `02_Datasets/raw_dataset.csv`. ### 2.2 Overview

The raw file has 508 rows and 12 columns [E1]. After cleaning it has 500 rows and 15 columns [E2]. Numeric outcomes are scores on a 0–100 scale and GPA on a 0–4 scale. Raw summary statistics are in [E1]; cleaned statistics are in [E2] and [E3].

### 2.4 Variables and Attributes

The primary outcome `final_score` has cleaned mean 57.74, standard deviation 8.62, median 57.80 and range 35.90–88.90 [E3]. `study_hours_per_week` has cleaned mean 11.92 (sd 4.76) after range correction and imputation; `attendance_percentage` has mean 81.40 (sd 9.83) [E3]. Categorical group sizes after cleaning include Pass 361, Fail 100, Credit 38, Distinction 1; Bachelor 192, High School 185, Master 86, PhD 37; and semesters 122, 121, 142, 115 [E3].

### 2.5 Collection Method

No human subjects were involved. The generator drew independent values from specified distributions (normal, truncated normal and categorical probabilities), constructed `final_score` as a linear function of study hours, attendance, prior GPA, parental education, extracurricular participation and internet access plus normal noise, then derived `midterm_score` from the final score with noise. It deliberately injected missing values, impossible ranges, extreme outliers, label variants and 8 duplicate rows so that the cleaning phase has observable defects to address. The fixed seed makes the entire raw file reproducible.

### 3.2 Collection

The raw CSV was produced by the deterministic generator described in 2.5 and then treated as immutable. Reproduce with `python 03_Source_Code/generate_raw_dataset.py`.

### 3.3 Cleaning and Preprocessing

All decisions and their row counts are in `cleaning_log.csv` [E2]:

- Removed 8 exact duplicate rows (508 → 500).
- Dropped `student_id` for privacy; no direct identifier remains.
- Unified 4 gender, 4 parental-education and 4 extracurricular label variants after whitespace stripping.
- Converted 3 negative study-hour values and 3 attendance values above 100 to missing.
- Flagged 6 study-hour outliers above the IQR upper bound of 24.91 hours.
- Imputed missing values with column medians: 24 study hours (median 12.10), 28 attendance (median 81.85), 20 prior GPA (median 2.93). Median was chosen over the mean for robustness to skew; row deletion was rejected because it would discard roughly 9% of records.
- Derived four variables with stated thresholds and validated the result: 500 rows, 15 columns, 0 missing, 0 duplicates [E2].

### 3.4 EDA

Descriptive statistics, frequencies, a correlation matrix with heatmap, group comparisons, distribution plots and a semester line plot were produced by `exploratory_analysis.ipynb` [E3]. Nine charts cover histogram, boxplot, bar, scatter, heatmap and line types. Each chart carries a title, labelled axes, a legend where applicable and a written interpretation.

### 3.5 Analytical Techniques

Phase 4 used: Pearson correlation; one-way ANOVA with eta-squared; Welch's t-test with Cohen's d; a mean-only dummy regressor as baseline versus linear regression with 5-fold cross-validation on training data; a near-duplicate midterm model reported only as a leakage warning; permutation importance; a majority-class dummy classifier versus logistic regression with stratified 5-fold cross-validation, F1, ROC AUC and a confusion matrix; and K-means for k=2–8 with inertia and silhouette diagnostics [E4]. An 80/20 `train_test_split(random_state=42)` was applied before fitting. No hyperparameter tuning was performed.

### 3.6 Visualization Methods

Static charts use matplotlib and seaborn (`04_Visualizations/*.png`, 12 files). Interactive charts use Plotly inside Streamlit (`04_Visualizations/dashboard/app.py`), with nine static PNG snapshots exported through Kaleido to `04_Visualizations/dashboard/snapshots/` [E5].

## Chapter 4 Results and Discussion

### 4.1 Dataset Summary

Raw: 508 rows × 12 columns, 8 duplicates, 60 missing numeric cells in total [E1]. Cleaned: 500 rows × 15 columns, 0 missing, 0 duplicates [E2]. The cleaned outcome distribution is unimodal and centred at 57.74 (sd 8.62) [E3].

### 4.2 Cleaning Results

The full action log is [E2]. The most consequential steps were removing 8 duplicates, dropping the identifier, correcting 12 impossible values, flagging 6 IQR outliers and imputing 72 missing cells with medians. Validation assertions passed, so the cleaned file is internally consistent. A known bias remains: median imputation shrinks variance and can attenuate relationships.

### 4.3 EDA

The strongest numeric associates of `final_score` are midterm score (r = 0.943), study hours (r = 0.696) and the composite risk index (r = -0.711); age is essentially uncorrelated (r = 0.005) [E3]. Group means are: attendance High 61.98, Moderate 57.75, Low 54.43; parental education Master 60.94, Bachelor 58.82, PhD 58.62, High School 54.95; extracurricular Yes 59.15, No 56.70 [E3]. Grade bands are imbalanced (72.2% Pass, 20.0% Fail, 7.6% Credit, 0.2% Distinction) [E3]. Semester means drift from 58.93 to 57.10; with groups of 115–142 and synthetic cross-sectional data, no trend is claimed [E3].

### 4.4 Statistical or Predictive Analysis

**Hypothesis tests** [E4]: study hours r = 0.696 (p = 1.3e-73) and attendance r = 0.306 (p = 2.5e-12) are significant and positive. Parental education ANOVA F = 12.362 (p = 8.25e-08, eta² = 0.0696) and attendance status ANOVA F = 21.791 (p = 8.48e-10, eta² = 0.0806) each explain under 9% of variance. Extracurricular participation gives t = 3.157 (p = 0.0017, Cohen's d = 0.287), a small effect. Significance partly reflects n = 500; effect sizes are the honest summary.

**Regression** [E4]: the mean-only baseline scores test R² = -0.011, RMSE 8.52, MAE 7.34. Linear regression without midterm scores R² = 0.703, RMSE 4.62, MAE 3.50, with 5-fold cross-validated R² = 0.723 and RMSE 4.478. A model including midterm reaches R² = 0.891 (RMSE 2.801), but the midterm is almost a restatement of the target (r = 0.943); this is reported only as a leakage warning and excluded from conclusions. Permutation importance for the main model is study hours 0.977, attendance 0.175, prior GPA 0.145 [E4].

**Classification** [E4]: the majority baseline reaches accuracy 0.800 but F1 0.000 because it never identifies a failing student. Logistic regression reaches accuracy 0.870, precision 0.733, recall 0.550, F1 0.629 and ROC AUC 0.914 (cross-validated F1 0.612). The confusion matrix `[[76, 4], [9, 11]]` shows 9 of 20 truly failing students are missed.

**Clustering** [E4]: silhouette scores range from 0.220 to 0.250, peaking at k = 4 (0.250). This is weak separation; no strong natural engagement profiles are claimed.

### 4.5 Visualizations

Twelve static charts are in `04_Visualizations/`: final-score histogram, study hours by grade band, parental-education means, study-hours scatter with fitted line, correlation heatmap, semester line, attendance-status means, extracurricular boxplot, grade-band counts, actual-versus-predicted, confusion matrix and clustering diagnostics. Every chart has a title, labelled axes, a legend where applicable and a 1–2 sentence interpretation in the notebook that generated it [E3, E4].

### 4.6 Dashboard

`streamlit run 04_Visualizations/dashboard/app.py` launches successfully (verified locally on port 8501). It provides four KPI cards, six sidebar filters (gender, parental education, extracurricular participation, semester, study-hour range, attendance range), nine interactive Plotly charts, a model-results table and a recommendations panel with an explicit synthetic-data caveat [E5]. Nine static PNG snapshots of the chart views were exported [E5].

### 4.7 Interpretation of Findings

Within this synthetic dataset, study behaviour is the dominant associate of final performance, and attendance adds a smaller but consistent association. Demographic group differences are statistically detectable but small in magnitude. A fail-detection classifier is better than a naive baseline but misses too many at-risk students for autonomous use. Clustering does not reveal strong segments. Because the outcome was constructed by a generator, these statements describe the data-generating process and the analysis pipeline, not real educational causation.

## Chapter 5 Conclusion and Recommendations

### 5.1 Summary of Findings

1. Cleaning converted 508 raw rows with defects into 500 validated, privacy-safe rows with four derived variables [E2].
2. Final scores centre on 57.74 (sd 8.62) with no ceiling artifact [E3].
3. Study hours (r = 0.696) and attendance (r = 0.306) are the leading modifiable correlates; demographic effects are small (eta² ≤ 0.081, d = 0.287) [E3, E4].
4. A transparent linear model explains 70.3% of test variance without leakage; the 89.1% model is invalid for conclusions because of midterm leakage [E4].
5. Fail detection reaches F1 0.629 but recall 0.550; 9 of 20 failing students are missed [E4].
6. Cluster structure is weak (best silhouette 0.250) [E4].
7. The dashboard reproduces the findings interactively with six working filters and nine charts [E5].

### 5.2 Conclusion

The project delivers a complete, reproducible analytics pipeline. Its strongest contribution is methodological transparency: every raw defect, cleaning action, statistical result and model metric is traceable to executed code. The analysis demonstrates how to avoid two common errors — reporting a leaked model as a success, and reporting accuracy as if it were sufficient under class imbalance.

### 5.3 Recommendations

1. **Prioritise study-support interventions over demographic targeting.** Study hours dominate both correlation (0.696) and permutation importance (0.977) [E3, E4].
2. **Monitor attendance as a secondary signal.** High versus Low attendance differs by 7.55 points [E3].
3. **Use the classifier only as a screening aid.** With recall 0.550, it should flag candidates for human review, not determine outcomes [E4].
4. **Report effect sizes alongside p-values.** Significant group tests explain under 9% of variance [E4].
5. **Exclude the midterm from predictive claims.** Any evaluation must document near-duplicate features [E4].

These recommendations are conditional on the data being synthetic; they illustrate how evidence should be used, not what should be done for real students.

### 5.4 Future Improvements

Replace the synthetic file with a real, ethically sourced dataset; collect a true time variable to support longitudinal analysis; reduce median-imputation bias with a principled missing-data method; use stratified sampling or threshold tuning (declared in advance) to improve at-risk recall; validate any cluster solution against an external criterion; and add automated notebook regression tests so reproducibility is checked on every change.
